# Worked Example

Abbreviated, to show the shape and the gap-finding. Details illustrative.

---

**Rep pastes:**

> Review this deal. Nucleus Software, $85K ARR, in Negotiation, close date end of month.
> Great champion in the VP of Ops, she loves us, we've done three demos. Only competition
> is a legacy tool they're sick of. Feeling good about this one.

**The skill reads it and finds the gaps behind the optimism. Output:**

> # Deal review: Nucleus Software — Real but at risk
>
> **Overall:** Real but at risk — thinner than "Negotiation" implies.
> **Biggest risk:** No economic buyer identified. Three demos in, the deal is running
> entirely through the VP of Ops, and nothing here shows who actually releases $85K.
> **Next move:** Get the VP of Ops to name and introduce the budget holder this week —
> if she can't or won't, that tells you what she really is.
>
> ## Hygiene
> - Stage vs. reality: sitting in Negotiation, but there's no proposal or pricing
>   mentioned and no economic buyer engaged. This looks like late-stage Discovery
>   mislabeled as Negotiation. The stage is flattering the deal.
> - Close date: end of month, but with no decision process established (below), that
>   date is a hope, not a plan.
> - Single-threaded: one contact across three demos. Top risk factor.
>
> ## MEDDICC gap-check
> - **Metrics:** GAP. "They're sick of the legacy tool" is a feeling, not a quantified
>   outcome. What does solving this save or make them? No number = no compelling event.
> - **Economic buyer:** GAP. The critical one. Never named. The VP of Ops may or may not
>   hold budget — unproven, and three demos deep that's late.
> - **Decision criteria:** THIN. Three demos suggest interest but no stated yardsticks.
>   What are they actually judging on?
> - **Decision process:** GAP. No steps to signature established. This is why the close
>   date is fiction, not a scheduling detail.
> - **Identify pain:** THIN. "Sick of the legacy tool" is real but mild. Is this urgent
>   enough to buy *now*, or coast-able? Mild pain doesn't close month-end.
> - **Champion:** THIN — and probably overrated. "She loves us" describes a coach, not a
>   champion. Has she sold you internally, brought others, pushed for budget? No evidence.
>   A responsive contact who hasn't moved anyone else is not yet a champion.
> - **Competition:** THIN. Named the legacy tool but missed the real competitor —
>   do-nothing. With mild pain and no economic buyer, "they keep limping along with the
>   old tool" is the likeliest outcome, and it isn't on the rep's radar.
>
> ## Stakeholders
> - VP of Ops — advocate, but unproven power, and the deal's only thread.
> - Everyone else — unknown. No other stakeholder has been engaged in three meetings.
> - The gap: single-threaded to a contact whose authority is unconfirmed. If she leaves,
>   goes quiet, or turns out not to hold budget, the deal is gone.
>
> ## Moves, ranked
> 1. **Close the economic-buyer gap.** Ask the VP of Ops directly: "Who else needs to be
>    part of this decision, and can you introduce me?" Her answer reveals both the buyer
>    and whether she's really a champion.
> 2. **Establish the decision process.** "Walk me through how a purchase like this
>    actually gets approved here." No real close date exists until you have this.
> 3. **Quantify the pain.** Turn "sick of the legacy tool" into a number — hours lost,
>    cost, risk. Without it, do-nothing wins.
> 4. **Multi-thread.** One relationship is the whole deal right now. Get a second.
>
> ## What I couldn't assess
> Economic buyer, decision process, and real decision criteria are all gaps because the
> data isn't there — not because the deal lacks them, but because they haven't been
> uncovered. That's the actual to-do list before this is a Negotiation-stage deal.

---

The signature: the rep came in feeling good; the review didn't argue with the feeling,
it found the four gaps underneath it — no economic buyer, no decision process, a
mislabeled "champion," and do-nothing as the unseen competitor — and turned each into a
specific next question. That's a deal saved a week early instead of a month-end surprise.

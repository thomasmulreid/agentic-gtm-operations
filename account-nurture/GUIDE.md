# Account Nurture — Guide

## What this is

A way to make keeping an account warm a short, concrete job instead of a vague "I should reach out to them" that keeps sliding down your list. The skill does the research you don't have time for — account news, changes on their side, what you've sent before, what's worth bringing them — and hands you a sourced, ready-to-edit touch aimed at a goal you choose. You review, tweak, and send. It never sends anything for you.

Built for account managers and customer success managers. What the touch is *for* — renewal, rapport, expansion, selling something new — is something you pick each time, so the same skill works whether you're protecting a renewal or growing an account.

## How a run works

**You name the account.** "Nurture Well.ca," "build a touch for Acme." If you don't name one, or there isn't enough for it to work from, it asks — which account, where the relationship stands, anything recent you know. It won't invent an account or run on nothing.

**You pick the goal.** Before it researches, it asks what this touch should move toward:

- **Renewal** — protect the account and tee up the renewal conversation
- **Rapport and trust** — pure value, no ask, just stay warm
- **Expansion** — grow within what they already have
- **Sell an additional product or service** — and it'll ask *which*, specifically

The goal changes what it researches and what the copy is allowed to do. A rapport touch won't end in a pitch. A renewal touch won't ignore the renewal.

**Then three stages, and you're in control at each gate:**

1. **It shows you what it found** — a slate of insights, each with a source, sorted into what's relevant, what's worth checking, and what it set aside. You confirm what's real and kill what's off. Nothing goes further until you sign off.
2. **It proposes approaches** — two or three ways to play the touch, just the strategy, not the copy. You pick one or blend.
3. **It writes the touch** — the actual email or Slack message, in your voice. You edit and send by hand. It records what you sent so next time doesn't repeat it, and offers to remind you when the next one's due.

## How it knows what you can sell

The first time you use it, it'll ask for your company website and read your product and services pages, so any cross-sell or expansion suggestion comes from what you actually sell — not a guess. If your offering isn't clear from the site, it'll ask you to name it rather than making something up.

## What it needs

Nothing required. It works from what you paste and what you tell it. If you've got a CRM, email, Slack, or a research tool connected, it'll use them to make the touch sharper — and it'll tell you honestly when the research is lighter because a source wasn't available. It won't pretend it looked somewhere it couldn't.

It also remembers. Over a few touches on the same account, it gets sharper — it knows what you already sent, which angle you already used, and won't repeat itself.

## The rules it follows so you don't get burned

- **Nothing it says about the account is made up.** Every claim is sourced from something it pulled this run, or it's flagged "worth checking" and left to you.
- **It never sends anything.** Every message is yours to send. The only thing it schedules is a reminder to you.
- **It aims at the goal you picked** and won't drift — no surprise pitch in a rapport touch, no ignored renewal in a renewal touch.
- **If it can't research much, it tells you** and gives you a lighter touch instead of inventing a reason to reach out.

## What good use looks like

When an account's been quiet too long, name it, pick the goal, and spend a few minutes: review the slate, pick an approach, edit the copy, send. The more you use it on an account, the better its read on that account gets.
